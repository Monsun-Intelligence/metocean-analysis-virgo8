Monsun Intelligence · Independent metocean assessment

What were the sea conditions during the KMP Virgo Transport 8 accident?

An independent look at wind, waves and currents around Masalembo / Laut Jawa — in the same spirit as our KM Putri Sakinah analysis.

This is an independent metocean assessment, not an accident investigation. It does not seek to determine the official cause of the capsizing or assign responsibility to any party.

1. The incident: what happened in Laut Jawa?

On the night of 12–13 September 2026, KMP Virgo Transport 8 — a Ro-Ro ferry on the Surabaya–Banjarmasin route — lost contact and capsized in the Java Sea near Masalembo. Public reports place the loss-of-contact / incident datum at approximately 4°31′51.82″S, 114°02′06.88″E (−4.531061, 114.035244), with the event time commonly cited near 02:00 WITA on 13 September (12 September 18:00 UTC). On the following day of SAR operations, the wreck was reported roughly 1.6 nautical miles west of that datum.

Weather and sea state were among the factors discussed in public coverage, including a BMKG characterisation of conditions as kategori sedang (moderate). From a metocean perspective, the useful question is narrower:

What were the actual environmental conditions around Masalembo when the incident occurred — and how did they evolve in the days before and after?

Monsun examined wind, waves and surface currents at the reported incident point and the estimated drift location, using regional INACAWO (~3 km / 1 h) together with NOAA GFS Wave / Atmos and HYCOM, for 10–16 September 2026.

2. Masalembo: an open-sea setting, not a sheltered channel

Unlike the island-sheltered waters of Labuan Bajo / Padar examined in the Putri Sakinah case, Masalembo sits in a comparatively open reach of the Java Sea. There is less topographic sheltering: regional wind and short wind-sea can organise over longer fetches, and there is less reason to expect a “calm shoreline / complex channel” contrast.

That geographic difference shapes how the numbers should be read. Here the story is less about a subtle local current anomaly in narrow passages, and more about whether a sustained moderate wind–wave regime was present on the open sea at the reported time — and what that implies for interpreting “cuaca sedang” in operational language.

3. What did the metocean data show?

Values below are sampled at the Basarnas-linked incident coordinates from INACAWO point time series, with GFS/HYCOM noted for cross-check. Full plots are in the meteogram and spatial viewer sections.

Wind: steady and moderate–fresh, not a calm night

Through 10–16 September, INACAWO 10 m wind at the incident point stayed mostly in the 15–22 kt band (about 8–11 m/s), averaging near 19 kt. At the reported event time (12 Sep 18Z), wind was about 19 kt from the ESE (~123°). GFS Atmos agrees closely: ~10.5 m/s (~20 kt) at the same valid time.

This is not an extreme storm signal — but it is also not a light, variable land–sea breeze regime. Direction was persistently from the east–southeast, consistent with a organised wind-sea over the Java Sea rather than a short-lived squall spike.

Waves: moderate significant height, rough individual extremes

Significant wave height (Hsig) at the incident point was about 1.6 m at 12 Sep 18Z (INACAWO scale: Moderate), with primary energy in the wind-sea (~1.6 m) and almost negligible primary swell at that hour. Peak period was near 6 s, with mean wave direction also from the ESE — aligned with the wind. GFS Wave SWH at the same time was ~1.55 m.

Over the full window, Hsig ranged roughly 0.6–1.9 m (mean ~1.5 m). Importantly, maximum wave height Hmax was about 3.2 m at event time (INACAWO scale: Rough), and reached up to ~3.7 m later in the window. As in any sea-state discussion: Hsig is a statistical descriptor; it does not equal the largest individual wave a vessel may encounter.

Day-mean Hsig rose from ~1.2 m on 11 Sep toward ~1.5 m on 12 Sep and ~1.7 m on 13–14 Sep — a gradual build rather than a single-hour jump. Peak Hsig in the extract actually occurred later (~15 Sep), so the reported incident sits inside an elevated, multi-day wind-sea episode, not at the absolute maximum of the series.

Current: present, but not the loudest signal

Surface current at event time was about 40–42 cm/s (~0.4 m/s, ~0.8 kt) toward the northwest in INACAWO, while HYCOM sampled nearer ~0.50 m/s at the same coordinates. Across 10–16 Sep, INACAWO speeds ranged from near-calm to about 70 cm/s, with a clear tidal-like modulation in direction (wide swings through the compass on 12–13 Sep) while magnitude stayed mostly in the tens of cm/s.

Currents matter for drift and heading relative to a vessel — and the reported ~1.6 NM westward displacement of the wreck is consistent with some westward component of motion after the event. But in this case, unlike the Putri Sakinah Labuan Bajo assessment where current stood out against modest wind and sub-0.5 m waves, the Virgo environment’s more distinctive signature is the sustained moderate wind-sea.

4. So, was the weather really the problem?

Based on the parameters examined, calling 12–13 September 2026 an “extreme weather event” at Masalembo would overstate what the hindcast shows. Wind near 19–20 kt and Hsig near 1.5–1.6 m sit in a moderate / rough individual-wave envelope — broadly compatible with public “kategori sedang” language — rather than a rare regional storm outlier.

At the same time, “not extreme” is not the same as “benign for every vessel.” A Ro-Ro ferry’s response depends on loading, stability, freeboard, heading relative to short wind-sea, speed/propulsion, and human factors. Metocean data can describe the forcing; they cannot, by themselves, prove how this particular vessel responded.

The environmental picture is a multi-day, wind-sea-dominated moderate regime on the open Java Sea — with Hmax locally exceeding 3 m — not a calm night and not a once-in-a-decade storm.

That distinction matters for operations and for investigation framing: weather may be a relevant contextual factor without being a sufficient explanation on its own.

5. The bigger lesson: why metocean intelligence matters

The broader lesson is the same one drawn from Putri Sakinah, even though the sea-state signature differs. Marine risk does not only appear as dramatic storms or multi-metre swell. It can sit inside “ordinary” seasonal conditions that are still operationally consequential for a given vessel and voyage.

For passenger ferries, cargo Ro-Ros, tourism craft and coastal logistics, environmental intelligence supports decisions about when to depart, which route to take, whether conditions remain suitable for continued operation, and when a weather window needs closer monitoring — using the right scale of data for the place.

Labuan Bajo taught that sheltered-looking water can hide complex current behaviour. Masalembo / Laut Jawa teaches the complementary point: on open reaches, a steady moderate wind-sea can be the primary environmental signal — and “sedang” still deserves quantitative scrutiny.

The ocean does not have to look extreme to become operationally important. What matters is knowing what the ocean was doing — and how that environment interacts with the operation taking place within it.

That is the purpose of metocean intelligence.

NOAA GFS Wave

Primary Gelombang

Model gelombang global yang digandeng GFS. Dipakai untuk tinggi gelombang signifikan (SWH), swell primer, arah gelombang, serta angin permukaan pada grid gelombang.

Metadata

Institusi / produk NOAA / NCEP — GFS Wave (global 0.25°)
Resolusi horizontal ~0.25° (~25–28 km di lintang ini)
Resolusi waktu 3 jam (siklus 00/06/12/18 + forecast hour)
Jendela plot 10 Sep 2026 00Z – 15 Sep 2026 21Z
Variabel yang dipakai swh, dirpw, swell, swdir, ugrdsfc, vgrdsfc
Unit plot peta SWH & swell: meter · arah: panah normalisasi

Cara data diambil

Unduhan historis dari bucket AWS Open Data noaa-gfs-bdp-pds. File GRIB2 gelombang gfswave.tHHz.global.0p25.fXXX.grib2 diunduh per valid time, lalu di-subset ke bbox analisis di lokal (scripts/download_gfs.py).

Caveat

  • Grid 0.25° merata-ratakan kondisi Laut Jawa terbuka di sekitar Masalembo; fitur mesoscale lokal bisa tersamar.
  • Densifikasi ke 0.01° pada peta hanya interpolasi + smoothing tampilan — tidak menambah informasi fisik.
  • Arah gelombang memakai konvensi meteorologi “dari mana datang”, lalu dikonversi ke vektor propagasi untuk panah.
  • Interpretasi SWH model vs laporan lapangan / BMKG harus mempertimbangkan resolusi, lokasi sampling, dan ketidakpastian laporan.

NOAA GFS Atmos (10 m wind)

Primary Angin

Komponen angin permukaan 10 m dari paket atmosfer GFS 0.25°, untuk meteogram dan peta angin.

Metadata

Institusi / produk NOAA / NCEP — GFS pgrb2 0.25°
Resolusi horizontal ~0.25°
Resolusi waktu 3 jam
Variabel u10, v10 (m/s; peta dikonversi ke knot)

Cara data diambil

Dari bucket yang sama (noaa-gfs-bdp-pds), file gfs.tHHz.pgrb2.0p25.fXXX. Sedapat mungkin memakai byte-range lewat file .idx agar hanya field u/v 10 m yang diunduh, lalu di-subset ke domain (scripts/download_gfs.py).

Caveat

  • Tidak menangkap variasi angin mesoscale di sekitar Pulau Masalembo / Laut Jawa dengan detail tinggi.
  • Angin GFS Atmos dan angin permukaan GFS Wave berasal dari paket berbeda; boleh berbeda tipis pada titik yang sama.
  • Forecast hour pendek dipakai untuk membangun deret historis valid-time.

HYCOM ESPC-D-V02

Primary Arus

Arus permukaan (lapisan terdekat permukaan) dari archive HYCOM ESPC-D-V02, untuk peta dan meteogram arus — relevan untuk drift bangkai yang dilaporkan Basarnas (~1.6 NM ke barat).

Metadata

Institusi / produk HYCOM Consortium — ESPC-D-V02 (u3z / v3z)
Akses THREDDS OpenDAP tds.hycom.org
Resolusi horizontal ~1/12° (orde ~9 km; tergantung grid native produk)
Resolusi waktu 3 jam (disample ke step konfigurasi)
Jendela plot (deliverable) 10 Sep 2026 00Z – 14 Sep 2026 21Z (15 Sep v3z timeout)
Variabel water_u, water_v, speed (m/s; peta ditampilkan cm/s)
Kedalaman Permukaan (depth ≈ 0, nearest)

Cara data diambil

Subset harian via NetcdfSubset (NCSS) ESPC-D-V02/u3z/{year} dan ESPC-D-V02/v3z/{year} untuk rentang waktu + bbox, digabung jadi NetCDF lokal (scripts/download_hycom.py). OpenDAP agregat tahunan sering hang; jika TDS/NCSS sementara down, jalankan ulang skript (file harian di data/hycom/raw/ di-resume).

Caveat

  • Arus model tidak sama dengan lintasan drift bangkai aktual; estimasi 1.6 NM barat adalah laporan operasional, bukan hasil adveksi model di halaman ini.
  • Sampling titik memakai interpolasi / nearest wet cell jika titik jatuh di mask.
  • Produk archive ESPC; bukan observasi in-situ. Verifikasi lapangan tetap diperlukan untuk investigasi forensik.

INACAWO (~3 km / 1 jam)

Primary · regional Gelombang · angin · arus

Model regional INACAWO untuk domain Masalembo / Laut Jawa. Dipakai sebagai produk utama viewer spasial dan sisi kiri perbandingan meteogram / direction rose.

Metadata

Produk INACAWO regional metocean
Resolusi horizontal ≈ 3 km (grid 161×161 ≈ 0.025°)
Resolusi waktu 1 jam
Jendela plot 10 Sep 2026 00Z – 16 Sep 2026 23Z
Variabel yang dipakai Hsig / WaveDir · PrimSwell / SwellDir · WindSpeed (+U/V) · CurrSpeed (+U/V) · Hmax · SST · SSS
Unit plot peta gelombang: meter · angin: knots · arus: cm/s · arah: panah
Path figure figures/spatial/inacawo/… · figures/point/inacawo/…

Cara data diambil

Paket titik (meteogram/rose/CSV) dan NetCDF spasial harian digabung (10–13 Sep + 13–16 Sep), lalu di-render ke peta Monsun lewat scripts/plot_inacawo_spatial.py.

Caveat

  • Produk model regional — bukan observasi in-situ; bandingkan dengan GFS/HYCOM pada waktu yang sama.
  • Unit angin pada field spasial diperlakukan sebagai knots (rentang ~0–25); arus native m/s dikalikan 100 untuk peta cm/s.
  • Overlap 13 Sep antara dua paket NetCDF di-deduplikasi pada sumbu waktu.

Caveat

Catatan metodologi tampilan. Field spasial GFS/HYCOM dapat di-densifikasi ke 0.01° (~1.1 km) dengan interpolasi linear + Gaussian ringan (refine_field.py) hanya untuk keterbacaan peta — bukan resolusi fisik baru.
